Lesson 1 · First steps: the Excel window

Workbooks, sheets, cells – and your very first formula

In this lesson you will learn to

  • recognise the main parts of the Excel window;
  • find a cell by its address (e.g. B3);
  • type text and numbers into cells and correct them;
  • write a first simple formula and save your work.

1. Workbook and worksheets

An Excel file (.xlsx) is called a workbook. A workbook contains one or more worksheets (sheets), shown as tabs at the bottom of the window (Sheet1, Sheet2, …). Each worksheet is a large grid of cells.

2. The Excel window

PartWhat it does
RibbonThe tabs at the top (Home, Insert, Formulas, …) with all the commands.
Name BoxTop-left box; shows the address of the selected cell.
Formula BarNext to fx; shows what is really stored in the selected cell (a value or a formula).
Column headingsLetters A, B, C, … across the top.
Row headingsNumbers 1, 2, 3, … down the left side.
Sheet tabsBottom-left; switch between worksheets, or add one with +.
Status barBottom edge; shows e.g. the sum and average of the selected cells.

3. Cells and cell addresses

Every cell has an address: its column letter followed by its row number. The cell in column C, row 3 is C3. Click any cell below – the Name Box shows its address and the Formula Bar its content, exactly as in Excel.

A group of cells is called a range and is written with a colon: B2:D4 means all cells from B2 to D4 (3 columns × 3 rows = 9 cells).

4. Entering and correcting data

  1. Click a cell and simply type. Press Enter to move down, or Tab to move right.
  2. To replace the content, select the cell and type again.
  3. To edit the content, double-click the cell or press F2.
  4. To delete the content, press Delete.
  5. Made a mistake? Ctrl+Z undoes it.

Excel recognises the type of what you type: text is aligned to the left, numbers to the right. If a number appears on the left, Excel has read it as text and cannot calculate with it.

Decimal separator. With Greek regional settings Excel expects a comma as decimal separator: type 0,25, not 0.25. (With English settings it is the other way round.) A quick check: the number must be right-aligned.
Units. Write units in the column heading – e.g. b (m) – and type only the number in the cells below. A cell containing 0.25 m is text, not a number.

5. Your first formula

A formula always starts with an equals sign =. In the example above, the cross-section area of beam B1 is calculated in cell E2 with

=B2*C2

i.e. A = b · h. We do not type the numbers 0.25 and 0.50 into the formula; we type the addresses of the cells that contain them. If you later change the width in B2, the area is recalculated automatically – this is the whole power of a spreadsheet.

The basic arithmetic operators are:

OperatorMeaningExample
+addition=A1+B1
-subtraction=A1-B1
*multiplication=A1*B1
/division=A1/B1
^power=A1^2

Tip: instead of typing B2, type = and then click on cell B2 with the mouse – Excel inserts the address for you.
